## Summary
The change adds `config reload -y` after restoring and comparing the saved `config_db.json`, before deleting its temporary backup (`tests/gnmi_benchmark/helpers.py:144–152`). It restores live CONFIG_DB and restarts subscriber services rather than merely restoring disk contents or suppressing the dbmemory sanity check.

The cleanup is correctly outside measured RPC/resource-sampling intervals. Existing ExitStack ordering deletes generated routes before restoration; restoration still executes when the removal callback raises. The new shell chain preserves the backup when reload fails, and `_restore_config` checks the resulting status.

Critically, the actual caller is wrapped by the **function-scoped `gnmi_tls` fixture**: its teardown rolls back the pre-TLS configuration checkpoint and waits for critical processes. Module sanity checks also include convergence handling. Claims that the next test/sanity has “no readiness protection,” or that the reload necessarily permanently loses all preexisting live settings, do not survive whole-caller grounding.

### Optional lifecycle hardening — not a defect finding
`helpers.py:149` uses raw reload rather than the common `config_reload` readiness lifecycle. In the pinned public CLI reference, a subsequent unforced reload can refuse while SwSS is young; the raw command is not a full convergence barrier. The outer TLS fixture attempts checkpoint rollback **before** its process wait, so readiness before rollback remains a useful hardening/verification target. Consider the common helper or an equivalent bounded readiness check in a follow-up, preserving backup retention on failure. Do not treat adding `-f` alone as sufficient recovery.

This is not a blocker or proven flaky benchmark: no supported-case failure was established, the normal next-case lifecycle has protection, and the external CLI revision is not proven to match the tested image. The author's additional wrapper reload hit a startup guard, but that wrapper is outside this diff; its failure does not establish that the benchmark's own cleanup failed.
